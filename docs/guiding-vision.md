# Guiding Vision for Thockdown

## Who this document is for
This document is strictly meant as development guidelines for you, **my trusty AI agent**. *This is not supposed to be read by a user or communicated to the user in any other way than through the spirit of our work and the finished product.*

## Who this project is for
The whole goal of Thockdown is to provide a **really, really neat** little thing to anyone who wants to use it and inject them with a tiny bit of **faith in humanity** - **and AI** for that matter. Faith, because someone out there is making something genuinely nice for them and AI is helping them do it. Something that is cute, but also really good at what it does. And maybe they feel that whoever made it has a lot of joy imagining them smile. Smile, because it's thoughtfully crafted at every turn and they understand that those who made it tried their very best to make it nice for them.

To that end, Thockdown is designed to be a perfectly customizable little space that people feel at home in. A space that makes them feel safe and happy and that they can retreat to whenever they need to be alone with their thoughts, be it for creative work, for keeping a diary, for maintaining a mental health journal or simply for looking through their memories as they listen to some comforting music. And this little space should be available to anyone (regardless of OS, older hardware or lacking technical background) at any time and at absolutely no cost - simply by opening their laptop or sitting down at their computer.

Thockdown should allow you to be productive, but never force you. Just make it easy if needed. There are no reminders, no pop ups, no ads, no demands, no distractions, just a cozy little space, tailored exactly to the user's comfort that allows them to get focused and typing whenever they want to with absolutely no friction. For those of them who struggle with procrastination, Thockdown is supposed to make it as easy to get started as possible. Maybe they just launch it to listen to a bit of music in mini mode, and maybe they just enjoy hearing the keys 'clack'. And before they even know it, they are happily typing their way out of a valley, solving what burdens them - or allowing them to share it with a note. Or maybe they just hit the keys in rhythm with the music. It's all good.

## How is this relevant to design?
Well, there is one main takeaway - Thockdown always has the user in mind first. That means:
- We treat the user well.
- We are mindful of their frustrations.
- We don't waste their time.
- We care about their happiness.
- We want to make the experience as enjoyable as possible.
- We want them to really feel that Thockdown is a gift worth cherishing, not thoughtless slop.
- We don't exclude anyone from that experience.
  - We make the app easy to use even for those with impaired vision.
  - We make sure the app runs on all major OS.
  - We make sure the app runs smoothly, even on older hardware.

## How do we apply that to development?
- The question "Is this good enough?" is naturally followed by the question "Is it the best possible experience we can deliver?" and/or "Would that make the user smile?"
- The question "Is it worth going the extra mile?" is always answered with "yes" eventually. We still prioritize tasks to deliver as much for the user as soon as possible, but we keep track of loose ends and fix them as soon as we can.
- We are really thorough in development and aren't comfortable abusing users as beta testers. Every bug that the user encounters pains us.
- We take the time for detail and continuously try to figure out what we can do to genuinely surprise the user with our thoughtfulness.
- When a user spends time with our app, our benchmark for success is the number of times they wonder "Why don't other apps do that? That's handled exactly the way it should be!"
